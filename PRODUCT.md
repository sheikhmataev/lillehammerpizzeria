# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

People in and around Lillehammer deciding where to eat, usually on a phone,
usually within the hour. Three real situations:

1. **The 19.40 decision.** Standing in the cold, one hand free, choosing
   between here and the three other places on Storgata. Needs: is it open,
   what is it, does it look good, how do I get in.
2. **The group.** A team after a match, a family of five, a birthday. One
   person is scrolling for everyone. Needs: is there something for the picky
   one, the vegan one, the kid.
3. **The regular.** Knows the place, wants a number and a phone call. Needs:
   the menu, fast, searchable.

Nobody arrives to read. Everyone arrives to decide.

## Product Purpose

Turn a 23-year-old family restaurant's real assets, a 108-dish menu, a room
that fills up, 953 Google reviews and a Turkish kitchen in a Norwegian ski
town, into a site that makes the decision easy and makes the place look worth
choosing. Success is a phone call, a booking, or someone walking into the
courtyard.

## Brand Personality

Generous, charred, hand-painted.

Not fine dining and not trying to be. This is a loud room with leather
booths, a grill, a pizza oven and a bar that stays open. The voice is short,
warm and plain. It never explains itself twice and never sells.

## Anti-references

- **Dark technical dashboards.** The first version of this site read as a
  developer tool: mono type everywhere, hairline rules, status HUDs. It was
  precise and it did not feel like a restaurant.
- **Cream and terracotta editorial.** The current default for every
  restaurant site and every AI-generated one. Off-white ground, display
  serif, italic pull quotes. Elegant and completely anonymous.
- **Endless single-page scroll.** Nine sections stacked on one page that
  nobody finishes.
- **Stock food photography and invented testimonials.** Everything shown is
  theirs, or it is drawn.

## Design Principles

1. **Open beats everything.** Whether the kitchen is on right now is the most
   valuable fact on the page. It drives the art direction, not a footer line.
2. **The number is the brand.** Dishes are numbered 1 to 202,
   non-contiguously, since 2003. People order by number. Set them large.
3. **Decide, do not read.** Every screen should let someone act. Long-form
   copy is a failure state.
4. **Drawn, not decorated.** Where a photograph would be weak, use the
   illustrated motif system. Never a gradient, never a colour block standing
   in for an image.
5. **Two colours, committed.** Red carries whole surfaces. Neutrals are
   support, never the point.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA. Body text at 4.5:1 minimum, verified rather than assumed, which
rules out white body copy on pure brand red. Full keyboard path through the
menu search and the dish selector. `prefers-reduced-motion` gets a real
alternative everywhere, not a disabled animation that leaves content hidden.
Norwegian is the primary language; copy is written for it first.
